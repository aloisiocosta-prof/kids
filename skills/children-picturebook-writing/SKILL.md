# Children's picturebook writing

## Required inputs

Use the user-selected age range first. The reference flow shows 0–2, 3–5, 6–9 and 10+. Do not choose for the user. Also collect the protagonist's approved name, age, interests, personality and cast.

## Story objective

Working title: "Uma Noite Mágica com Papai Noel". Theme: courage. Setting: a magical Christmas Eve. Build a new story and original supporting characters; do not reuse Duda, Avó Lúcia, Mégui or the reference's prose/plot unless the user independently supplies those as their own characters.

## Courage treatment

- Courage is compatible with fear.
- Let the protagonist recognize and name the emotion.
- Model asking a trusted person for help, planning, pausing, listening and trying one safe step.
- A meaningful action by the protagonist should help the group resolve the problem.
- Adults remain responsible for safety and do not pressure the child to take risks.
- Never equate bravery with obedience to strangers, secrecy, physical danger or emotional suppression.

## Narrative architecture

Use a clear arc: familiar world and protagonist identity; magical invitation and understandable problem; emotional hesitation and naming the feeling; trusted support and safe plan; small attempts, clues and cooperative action; satisfying resolution and recognition; reflection, shared warmth and closure.

Use concrete actions, sensory details, recurring motifs (e.g. bell, star or lantern), causal links and page-turn questions. A recurring motif must have a defined role in the resolution.

## Age adaptation

- **0–2:** one simple idea per page, very short repetitive lines, predictable sequence, concrete words and strong visual support.
- **3–5:** short sentences, familiar vocabulary, clear emotional labeling, repetition and simple cause/effect.
- **6–9:** richer dialogue and motivations, a few linked clues, independent problem-solving with clear support.
- **10+:** greater nuance, layered motives and figurative language while retaining clear pacing and a child-credible point of view.

These are editorial defaults, not developmental diagnoses. Review the final draft with the family/educator.

## Page writing contract

Every narrative page includes page number and beat title; final child-facing prose; narrative purpose and emotional beat; visible action not already obvious from adjacent pages; illustration brief; continuity facts; narration text and pause cues if applicable.

Avoid overcrowded prose. The illustration should add or extend information rather than merely repeat each sentence.

## Review

Check meaningful protagonist choices, safe resolution, emotional acknowledgement, coherent cause/effect, page-turn logic, and a satisfying ending. Do not use a moralizing lecture in place of character action.
